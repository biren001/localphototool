How the transfer works

Open this page on both devices — for example your phone and your computer. One device shows a six-character code and a QR code; the other scans it or types the code in. The two browsers then negotiate a direct, encrypted peer-to-peer connection using WebRTC, the same technology video call sites use. From that moment the files flow straight from one device to the other.

This is the same promise as the rest of the site: nothing is uploaded. There is no server in the middle holding your photos, and there is no account anywhere. The connection details are exchanged through a signaling service — the operator that connects the call — and once the direct link is up, it is not involved any more.

When you actually need this

  • Right after editing here. Compressed a photo on your phone and want to keep working on the desktop — send the result over in one tap, at original quality.
  • Bulk phone-to-PC moves. Offload a camera roll after a trip without a cable and without trusting a cloud folder.
  • Sensitive files. Documents, ID scans, anything you would rather not hand to a cloud provider — a direct link means there is no third copy anywhere.
  • Quick text handoff. A link, a Wi-Fi password, a tracking number — type it on one device, paste it on the other.

Frequently asked questions

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. The devices connect through an encrypted WebRTC link: when the two networks allow it, the files travel straight between them, byte for byte. If both networks are too strict for a direct link, the encrypted stream is relayed through a public TURN relay so the page still works — the relay only forwards already-encrypted bytes, cannot read the content, and never stores anything. What no server ever receives is unencrypted data.

What is the signaling service, and what does it see?

Before two devices can talk directly, they have to find each other — like an operator connecting a call. A public signaling service exchanges the connection details (network addresses and encryption keys) between your two devices. It can see that two browsers are connecting, and nothing about what they send afterwards. File bytes never touch it.

Is there a file size limit?

No hard limit. Files are streamed in small chunks, so even large videos transfer. Very large files take proportionally longer, and the speed depends on the slower of the two connections — the data goes over your networks, not through our servers.

Does it work if the phone is on mobile data and the computer is on Wi-Fi?

Sometimes — but treat it as best-effort. On the same Wi-Fi network the connection is always reliable. Across networks, the two devices must first punch a direct link through both routers, and whether that succeeds depends on how strict each network is: many home broadband lines and most corporate networks make it fail. When a direct link and the encrypted relay both fail, the transfer stops with a clear message rather than hanging.

Is the transfer quality-lossless?

Yes — the files arrive byte for byte identical to the originals. Nothing is re-encoded, resized or recompressed. If you want a smaller version first, compress the photo on this site before sending it.

Does anything stay on the page after I close it?

No. The pairing code, the messages and the files exist only inside the browser tab while it is open. Refresh or close the tab and everything is gone — there is no history and no account.
